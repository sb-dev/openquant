# Wesley R. Gray and Jack R. Vogel, Quantitative Momentum: A Practitioner's Guide to Building a Momentum-Based Stock Selection System — summary-derived method notes

Source: summary PDF `1df0bec55706`, recorded in `packages/agents/src/methods/sources.json`. Draft; no rule has been checked against the book.

## Scope

This source gives the proposer a skip-month cross-sectional momentum family, a path-smoothness rank and a seasonal rebalance schedule. It gives the critic checks on lookback alignment, return basis, sort direction, fill timing, rebalance accounting, schedule-failure handling and portfolio-evidence transfer. The skip-month price ratio, eligibility, one descending rank, bounded slots and a declared schedule fit the initial platform; up/down session counts, percentile screens, beta, security-type data and a benchmark overlay need reviewed operators or data. Valuation screens need reviewed fundamental data; pairing this family with a separate value strategy, like any account-level allocation, is out of scope.

## Conflicts and adaptations

- Return basis: the summary computes momentum from dividend- and split-adjusted closes; OQ-02 §2.6 allows only causal split-adjusted signal prices and books dividends separately. A price-only measure is a different signal that ranks high-yield names lower, and ex-dividend sessions can count as down sessions.
- Sign operator: the ID formula multiplies by the sign of past return, and no reviewed sign operator exists. Make positive skip-month momentum an eligibility condition and rank descending on up-minus-down share, using the single descending rank and ascending instrument-ID tie-break. The counts themselves still need a reviewed operator.
- Nested funnel: a top-decile momentum screen followed by a top-half path-quality screen cannot be written with one descending rank. Either request a reviewed cross-sectional percentile operator or declare a single-rank version as a different strategy; ranking all positive-momentum names by smoothness ignores momentum strength.
- Portfolio construction: the summary's 50 equal-weight names with quarterly turnover differ from the seed's five slots and vacant-slot sizing of strategy cash. Continuing holdings are not reset to equal weight, sizing never uses account-wide capital, and exit proceeds cannot fund entries at the same open.
- Lookback depth: a 252-session lag and a 253-bar warm-up assume the reviewed bounded-lag operator admits that depth. If it does not, the family needs a reviewed extension, not a shorter window published under the same name.
- Relation to the seed: the seed's `close > SMA50` is a time-series trend test and its rank rewards volume surges and low RSI. Adding a skip-month momentum condition to the seed is a controlled revision with a parent hash; a full momentum funnel is a new family without a parent.
- Benchmarks: a trend-overlay benchmark is a strategy data dependency and stays distinct from the evaluation policy's benchmarks. Adding the overlay is a separate registered revision and cannot change the comparison conditions.
- Schedule failures: the summary's quarterly schedule leaves late or missing decision inputs unhandled; GRAYVOGEL-17 sets the declared behaviour and the external-dependency rule.
- Thresholds: the summary's percentiles, holding count and calendar are strategy parameters. Acceptance thresholds, evaluation windows and finding treatment come from the registered evaluation policy, never from this source.
- Skew: Carver's summary links trend rules to positive skew (CARVER-13); Chan's calls momentum positively skewed yet crash-prone (CHAN-10); this summary warns of crashes (GRAYVOGEL-14); Kaufman's says stocks favour short-term mean reversion. Time-series trend and cross-sectional momentum differ, as do horizons; skew and persistence are measured on registered data, never assumed.
- Other notes: momentum ranks derived from the Chan note (CHAN-03) use the same lagged-close arithmetic on causal split-adjusted closes, with declared lags and skip window; neither note's rank may be presented as this summary's total-return measure (GRAYVOGEL-02). Agreement between summaries verifies neither book.

## Limits

- Excluded summariser material: the opening AI-skill framing, the instruction to use its formulas verbatim, the p3 interactive visualiser section, whose one sentence promises an absent visual, and the closing reader question on p4.
- The p3–p4 guardrails addressed to an AI skill, including the trend overlay, are `summariser_recommendation` material or context; only the bear-market point credited to Chapter 8 and Appendix A is an unverified book claim, noted in GRAYVOGEL-14 and GRAYVOGEL-15.
- Rendering repairs only: raw LaTeX commands and escaped percent and underscore characters on p2–p4 were read as plain symbols; `12_2` and `RET_{2,12}` name the same quantity. No repair is a primary-page check.
- Inconsistent example: the p2 path example of 180 up and 70 down sessions totals 250 sessions, close to a full year, while the text calls it 11 months, about 231 sessions. Primary check needed.
- Unverified numbers: funnel sizes, the 15%, 10% and 5% cut-offs, top decile, top half, 50 holdings at 2%, the 21- and 252-session lags, the 252-session ID window, the rebalance months and the 12-month overlay average.
- Overstated claims treated as unverified: exclusive continuation at the intermediate horizon, value plus momentum lowering drawdowns and raising Sharpe ratios, momentum concentration in quarter-end months, and window dressing and tax-loss selling as causes.
- Attributions not checked: the 2014 Da, Gurun and Warachka origin of ID, the chapter ranges given for principles and pipeline, and whether the daily lag form is the book's or the summariser's approximation.
- The summary labels the beta and outlier stage optional; whether the book treats it as part of its core selection is unverified.
