# David Aronson, Evidence-Based Technical Analysis: Applying the Scientific Method and Statistical Inference to Trading Signals — summary-derived method notes

Source: summary PDF `231fba17dc73`, recorded in `packages/agents/src/methods/sources.json`. Draft; no rule has been checked against the book.

## Scope

This summary supports evaluation discipline in both pilot skills: objective rule statements, causal timing, session alignment, registration of every trial, search-adjusted inference, exposure-matched benchmarks and holdout consumption. The proposer uses it to write falsifiable definitions, invalidation conditions and requested tests; the critic uses it to detect selection bias, leakage and unsupported significance claims. Short or ternary positions and log-return accounting are outside the initial platform, and the summary's resampling tests need a reviewed declaration in the registered policy before use. No rule here sets a threshold, an acceptance gate or an independent-trial count.

## Conflicts and adaptations

- The summary sets positions from data at the close of t-1 and credits them with r_t from P_{t-1} to P_t (p3, p5); read as closing prices, that implies a fill at the signal close. OpenQuant fills entries at the next open, so recompute any reproduced statistic from next-open fills.
- The summary treats log returns as the accounting basis (p3, p6). OpenQuant ledgers compound simple returns and add positions in currency; log, detrended and winsorised series are labelled diagnostics.
- Lint rule 6 (p7) and Stage 5 (p6) mark a holdout in-sample only once a failed test leads to changes, and the lint rule accepts a permutation re-test instead of a new holdout. OQ-02 §3.3 consumes it on reveal; only new data or prospective paper evidence is fresh.
- Stage 3 (p5) counts raw variants as K; OQ-02 §3.1 forbids reading a precise independent-trial count from raw jobs. Joint resampling and a conservative Bonferroni bound may use the number of distinct registered variants; an independence-based correction or deflated-Sharpe trial count may not treat it as independent.
- The alpha levels, resample counts and promotion step (p4–p6) belong to the registered evaluation policy (OQ-02 §§3.1, 3.6). A method note cannot set a gate, a blocking treatment or a threshold.
- p4 computes the Reality Check p-value without the plus-one correction it uses for the permutation p-value. A policy that declares both tests should choose one convention and record it.
- The summary's rules hold -1, 0 or +1 positions. The initial platform is long-only, so a ternary source rule becomes a different long-or-flat strategy with a recorded interpretation change.
- The summary's market return ln(P_t/P_{t-1}) does not state a price basis. Signals use causal split-adjusted prices and dividends are accounted separately; a benchmark or detrending series must declare whether it includes dividends.
- The summary does not define RSI or ATR smoothing (p2, p7). Keep the seed's 14-bar simple-mean operators; Wilder or EMA versions need different operator identities. The weekday condition in lint rule 8's example needs an operator the initial set lacks.
- The permutation test (p4) pairs fixed signals with shuffled returns; the bootstrap resamples whole sessions of rule returns. Seed ranking, cash, stops and targets depend on the price path, so rerun the joint policy on permuted prices or declare the approximation, and bootstrap its daily returns in blocks (OQ-02 §2.4).
- This summary presents data mining as the only valid route to objective signals (p1), yet gives mined rules lacking a mechanism a low prior (p2). The Carver and Chan summaries require a stated mechanism first (`CARVER-01`, `CHAN-01`).
- Resolution: hypotheses may come from source methods, seed rules, component-removal tests and prior failures (OQ-03 §2). A mined rule is a registered trial needing search adjustment (`ARONSON-04`, `ARONSON-05`), and a missing mechanism is `hypothesis_assumption` however the rule was found.

## Limits

- Excluded summariser material: the validation-layer framing and the bullet label banning subjective analysis (p1), the five-stage state machine (p4–p6) and the eight lint rules as mandatory actions (p6–p7). Rules whose substance comes only from them are `summariser_recommendation`.
- The Chapter 2 catalogue of cognitive biases (p1–p2) gives no testable procedure, so it supplies no rule here beyond the falsification emphasis in `ARONSON-01`.
- Rendering damage: raw LaTeX in the Chapter 3 workflow arrows (p2), the Chapter 8 p-value (p2) and the formulas on p3–p7. Repairs here are readings of the summary, not primary checks.
- Corrections still needing a primary-page check: the growth rate of selection bias, the bootstrap algorithm, the permutation p-value wording and the list of price-motion mechanisms.
- Unverified quotation: the summary attributes to the book a short Popperian phrase condemning subjective analysis (p1); primary check needed.
- The claim that error variance shrinks as 1/N (p3) needs a finite long-run variance: short-memory dependence changes only the constant, while long memory can slow the rate. Unverified against the book.
- Unverified numbers: 6,402 rules; daily S&P 500, 1980–2004; hundreds of naively significant rules; 5,000 draws; at least 1,000 permutations; alpha 0.05 or 0.01; the 80% long-exposure example; publication year 2006. Edition unknown.
- The summary tests rules on one market series, singly or as a K-rule family. It gives no method for ranking, slot sizing, stops, targets or break-even moves, so no rule here changes the seed's exits or sizing.
- Summary-only rules can support drafts and test cases but cannot enter a released bundle (OQ-03 §3). None is evidence of profitability.
