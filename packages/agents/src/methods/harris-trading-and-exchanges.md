# Larry Harris, Trading and Exchanges: Market Microstructure for Practitioners — summary-derived method notes

Source: summary PDF `4de1682cd1c5`, recorded in `packages/agents/src/methods/sources.json`. Draft; no rule has been checked against the book.

## Scope

This source supplies microstructure questions for both pilot skills: who pays a strategy's expected profit, whether a dip reflects information or temporary liquidity pressure, how to measure the cost between decision and fill, and why touch-based limit fills flatter results. The initial platform has daily OHLCV only, with no quotes, trade signs or order book. Spread decompositions, venue and order-handling tactics and market-design topics therefore fall outside the initial platform, and limit-price entries would need a reviewed operator. Spread and slippage enter only as cost assumptions of the registered evaluation policy.

## Conflicts and adaptations

- The summary's spread and price-impact formulas assume quotes, trade signs and intraday timestamps. The initial platform has daily OHLCV only, so skills treat spread and slippage as declared cost assumptions of the registered policy and never request or invent quote data.
- Implementation shortfall maps onto daily runs with the signal close as decision price and the raw next open as fill. Signal prices are split-adjusted and fills are raw, so convert to one share basis first; the delay term then also absorbs impact, which daily data cannot separate.
- The summary's shortfall formula is buy-only. Exits need D = -1; totals that mix entries and exits without a stated sign convention net costs against gains.
- OQ-02 §2.5 fills a target when the high touches it, the same touch assumption the summary calls naive for limit orders. A critic may request a touch-only diagnostic now; a trade-through stress needs separately versioned fill semantics, and neither role can change the declared fill model.
- The summary's midpoint option cannot apply to daily bars; its executable-price option maps onto the declared per-side spread cost at the next-open fill. Signals stay on causal split-adjusted closes and fills on raw opens (OQ-02 §2.6). A typical-price or VWAP substitute is a different operator identity.
- The summary favours call auctions for opening or illiquid trades. OpenQuant's next-open fill is a declared simulation assumption (OQ-02 §2.5), not evidence that an opening auction would fill that size at that price.
- When stop and target are both touched and their order is unknown, the stop takes precedence (OQ-02 §2.5). Microstructure arguments about which level traded first cannot override this without intraday evidence.
- The summary's order-size guidance addresses account-level institutional orders. Strategy sizing divides unreserved cash within its assigned budget by vacant slots, with lot rounding, and never uses account-wide capital (OQ-02 §2.4, §2.6).
- Thresholds in the summary, such as the cut-off of 10 percent of average daily volume (ADV) or the claim that, for fast-decaying signals, a missed fill costs more than the spread, are not acceptance or sizing thresholds. The registered evaluation policy owns those.
- Cost levels in other sources' rules do not override the registered evaluation policy. Each cost stress belongs to the declared robustness suite or a separately registered comparison.
- Volume readings conflict: `MURPHY-07` reads above-average volume on recovery bar t as the trend resuming, while `HARRIS-03` treats high volume as possibly marking an informed, permanent move. The strategy contract must choose one reading and state the condition that would invalidate it (OQ-02 §2.1).

## Limits

- Excluded: the p6 simulator heading, which contains no simulator, the closing reader question offering to build an AI skill, and the p5 advice never to display full size.
- Downgraded to summariser material: the p5 order and venue decision matrix, the p6 list of checks an AI skill should flag, and section 6 generally. Rules resting on them carry `summariser_recommendation`.
- The summary says the book distils into six modules, but its sixth section is its own AI-skill blueprint, not book content.
- Unverified numbers and citations: 29 chapters, Chapter 20, Chapters 4–6, part assignments that overlap between sections, the cut-off of 10 percent of average daily volume (ADV) and the 100 percent adverse-selection claim.
- The three-part spread split (processing, inventory, adverse selection) is common in textbooks, but the book's own component count is unverified; primary check needed. No rule relies on it, because daily data cannot measure it.
- Rendering repairs: raw LaTeX in the p4–p5 formulas is restated in plain symbols in `HARRIS-07` and `HARRIS-08`; the p3 spread decomposition is described in words only. Units, trade-sign classification and the post-trade interval remain unstated in the summary.
- Corrections still needing a primary-page check: `HARRIS-01` zero-sum scope, `HARRIS-02` value traders and dealer losses, `HARRIS-03` chapter, `HARRIS-05` orthogonality, `HARRIS-07` spread formulas, `HARRIS-08` shortfall form, `HARRIS-12` adverse selection, `HARRIS-13` stop-hunting, `HARRIS-14` limit-order option and `HARRIS-15` precedence attribution.
- Overstatements softened: execution described as wholly counterparty-dependent, dealers as always losing to informed traders, models as certain to invent reversal profits, and 100 percent adverse selection for back-of-queue fills.
- Market-design topics produce no rules: the four market-quality goals (liquidity and low costs overlap), the liquidity versus price-efficiency trade-off, tick size and pennying, and order precedence.
- No rule here is evidence of profitability or an acceptance threshold. Rules with `seed` scope supply questions and requested tests; finding treatment comes from the registered policy.
